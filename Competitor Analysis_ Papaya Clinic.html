1. My client: Papaya ClinicClient

Website · Instagram · Facebook

Overview

Papaya Clinic is an integrative women’s-health clinic in Newtown, Sydney. It supports women through pelvic health, fertility, pregnancy, postpartum recovery, hormonal health, PMS, PMDD, menopause and general wellbeing. Its model combines physical, hormonal, nutritional and emotional support.

Location: Suite 6, 32–72 Alice Street, Newtown, NSW 2042. Products can be collected from the Newtown and Leichhardt clinics, and delivery is also available. Established: 2023.

Founder, mission and social presence

Papaya was founded by Winnie Wu, a women’s-health physiotherapist and pelvic-health practitioner. It aims to improve access to pelvic-health care and provide patient-first, collaborative support for women throughout life, integrating physical, hormonal, nutritional and emotional healthcare in one clinic.

Its Instagram and Facebook are active, with content on women’s-health education, pelvic health, fertility, pregnancy, menopause, practitioner information, community events and collaborative care.

Courses, workshops and digital

Papaya runs community nights and roughly 90-minute workshops on fertility, menopause, acupuncture, yoga, nervous-system regulation and women’s-health conditions. Winnie Wu also provides professional mentoring and clinical leadership.

Hybrid-health programs combine online education, in-clinic care, movement sessions and accountability support, designed to improve health literacy, confidence and long-term behaviour change.

Physical products

The broadest physical-product shop in this comparison:

  • Wearable TENS devices and TENS machine hire
  • Pelvic-floor weights and pelvic wands
  • Vaginal dilators and lubricants
  • Postpartum, pregnancy and incontinence garments
  • Pilates balls, discs, socks and equipment
  • Foam rollers, massage tools and recovery products
  • Intimate-care products, supplements and postpartum support items
  • Children’s and adult Archies footwear

Pricing (main items)

  • Holistic Health Programs: $220 deposit + $3,180 or $223/week for 16 weeks; or $220 deposit + $1,280 or $121/week for 12 weeks.
  • Physiotherapy (Leichhardt and Newtown): initial 45 min $185, review 30 min $145, pessary fitting $120 on top of consultation fees.
  • TENS machine hire for labour, periods and pelvic pain: 2 weeks $38, 4 weeks $50, 6 weeks $60 (plus $12 for electrodes).
  • Pre Pointe and dance assessments: $220. Rise to Pointe 6-week program: $300.
  • Clinical Exercise: new patient bundle (assessment + 2 privates) $455, ongoing privates $170 for 50 min, duet $90 per person, 5-pack $690.
  • Massage: 45 min $145, 60 min $160, 90 min $230. Lymphatic drainage 75 min $210.
  • Naturopathy, nutrition and acupuncture: 20-minute express consults $60; initial and review sessions from $120 to $190.

Audience

Women across different life stages, particularly aged 25–60; pregnant and postpartum women; women with pelvic pain, fertility, PCOS, PMS, PMDD, hormonal or menopause concerns; and customers seeking coordinated physical, emotional and nutritional care.

Results and testimonials

4.9 stars from 52 Google reviews. Reviewers describe warm, careful and considered practitioners and feeling well cared for.

Strengths

  1. Broadest multidisciplinary women’s-health model.
  2. Strong local Sydney and Inner West presence.
  3. Physical shop connected to clinical care.
  4. Support across fertility, pregnancy, postpartum, menopause and pelvic health.
  5. Community workshops and hybrid programs.
  6. Strong patient-first and collaborative positioning.
  7. Instagram content is relevant to the clinic’s services.

Weaknesses

  1. Smaller social-media audience than Female Physio Co.
  2. Public pricing is less visible than Sunrise’s.
  3. The broad range of services and products may make the brand difficult to understand.
  4. Product education and shop promotion could be stronger on Instagram.

2. Sunrise Pelvic PhysiotherapyCompetitor

Website · Instagram

Overview

A specialist pelvic-health clinic in Newtown, Sydney, focused on bladder and bowel conditions, prolapse, pelvic-floor dysfunction, sexual pain, pelvic pain, pregnancy and postnatal recovery. Established in 2021.

Founder, mission and social presence

Created by Tusanee “Tus” Jierasak, a pelvic physiotherapist, as a safe and empowering space to discuss sensitive pelvic-health concerns. Its mission is to help clients build confidence and embrace pelvic health throughout life, with evidence-based, holistic, compassionate, inclusive and trauma-informed care.

It has an Instagram account and Facebook page, with positioning on specialist pelvic-floor physiotherapy.

Courses and digital

Pelvic-health education, pregnancy education, birth preparation, postnatal guidance and community education, shared through its website and social media. No major paid e-course library was identified. Main digital resources are website information, FAQs, blogs, online booking and social-media education.

Physical products

No dedicated shop was visible on the website. Sunrise gives clinical advice about pelvic-health products, but it does not appear to have a product catalogue comparable with Papaya or Female Physio Co.

Pricing

  • Pelvic health: initial consultation (pelvic physiotherapy, pregnancy or postnatal, 60 min) $220. Follow-ups: 60 min $195, 45 min $165, 30 min $135.
  • General musculoskeletal and pregnancy physiotherapy: initial 60 min $175. Follow-ups: 60 min $170, 45 min $150, 30 min $130.
  • Mastitis and blocked ducts: initial 60 min $185 or 45 min $170. Follow-ups: 45 min $140, 30 min $130. Emergency out-of-hours mastitis services carry an additional surcharge.

Audience

Women and people with pelvic-floor, bladder, bowel, prolapse, sexual-pain or pelvic-pain concerns; pregnant women; new mothers; and customers seeking specialist and confidential care.

Results and testimonials

Reviews praise knowledgeable, gentle practitioners and clear explanations.

Strengths

  1. Very clear pelvic-health niche.
  2. Strong clinical specialisation.
  3. Trauma-informed and supportive care.
  4. Strong pregnancy and postnatal services.
  5. Transparent consultation pricing.
  6. Local Newtown location.
  7. Social content has a clear clinical focus.

Weaknesses

  1. Narrower service range than Papaya.
  2. Mainly focused on physiotherapy.
  3. No visible e-course or product ecosystem.
  4. Smaller digital reach than Female Physio Co.
  5. Less ability to provide psychology, nutrition, naturopathy or acupuncture through the same clinic.

Overview

An evidence-based women’s-health physiotherapy business in Robina on the Gold Coast. It provides clinical physiotherapy, pregnancy and postnatal care, pelvic-health treatment, Pilates, telehealth, online programs and physical products. Established around 2020. Telehealth lets it serve customers outside Queensland.

Founder, mission and social presence

Founded by Sarah Percy, a Doctor of Physiotherapy, women’s-health physiotherapist, Pilates instructor and clinic director. The business aims to make evidence-based women’s-health and pelvic-floor education more accessible, with a focus on empowerment and practical education.

Its Instagram profile links to community, Pilates, team, subscription, booking, clinic, pregnancy, appointments, products and daily tips, creating a clear path from free education to appointments, programs and purchases.

Courses and digital

Pilates, pelvic-floor challenges, pregnancy exercise programs, postnatal exercise programs and public educational content. A formal professional mentoring program was not clearly identified. It has the strongest digital-product range:

eBook and program prices are public (see above). Clinical consultation prices were not clearly available online.

Audience

Australian and international women seeking accessible pelvic-floor education; pregnant women; new mothers; women with incontinence, prolapse, pelvic pain or menopause symptoms; and customers who prefer online programs, telehealth or self-guided exercise resources.

Results and testimonials

5.0 stars on Google. Reviewers describe feeling comfortable, listened to and well informed.

Strengths

  1. Much larger social-media audience than Papaya.
  2. Strong founder-led personal brand.
  3. Highly engaging short-form educational content.
  4. Clear evidence-based positioning.
  5. Strong online course and eBook ecosystem.
  6. Transparent digital-product pricing.
  7. Telehealth expands its geographic reach.
  8. Instagram connects education, booking, subscriptions, Pilates and products.

Weaknesses

  1. Physical clinic is located on the Gold Coast, not Sydney.
  2. Large online audience may not directly convert into local appointments.
  3. Brand depends heavily on Sarah Percy’s personal profile.
  4. Mostly physiotherapy-led, with fewer disciplines than Papaya, although it also offers a dietitian, Pilates and massage.
  5. Digital programs cannot replace individual assessment for complex conditions.

End summary

Key findings

  • Papaya Clinic is a multidisciplinary women’s-health clinic in Newtown and Leichhardt, Sydney, offering pelvic-health physiotherapy, pregnancy and postpartum care, fertility support, menopause services, acupuncture, naturopathy, nutrition, psychology, massage and clinical Pilates.
  • Sunrise Pelvic Physiotherapy is Papaya’s closest local specialist competitor. Its key advantage is specialist clarity and transparent pricing.
  • Female Physio Co. is the strongest digital competitor. It operates from the Gold Coast but reaches a national and international audience through telehealth, online programs, eBooks, Pilates and social media.
  • Papaya has the broadest physical-product range, including pelvic-health tools, TENS devices, dilators, pelvic wands, postpartum garments, Pilates equipment, massage tools, intimate-care items and supplements.
  • Female Physio Co. has the strongest digital-product ecosystem, with low-cost programs, guides and bundles from $19 to $199.
  • Sunrise has the clearest public consultation pricing, with an initial pelvic-health consultation at $220 for 60 minutes and follow-ups from $135 to $195. Papaya’s full fee structure is less visible, and Female Physio Co.’s clinical consultation prices were not clearly available online.
  • Customer feedback is positive across all three. Common themes are compassionate practitioners, feeling listened to, detailed assessments, clear explanations, personalised treatment plans and improved confidence.

Client’s unique strengths

  • Most comprehensive service model: physiotherapy, psychology, nutrition, naturopathy, acupuncture, massage and clinical Pilates support physical, hormonal, nutritional and emotional health in one organisation.
  • Strong local advantage: Newtown and Leichhardt locations give a stronger face-to-face advantage in Sydney than Female Physio Co.
  • Integrated patient journey: support across fertility, pregnancy, postpartum recovery, pelvic pain, PMS, PMDD, hormonal concerns and menopause.
  • Strong physical shop: products recommended through the clinical team link treatment, education and sales.
  • Community-based brand: workshops, community nights and events position Papaya as a women’s-health community.
  • Patient-first positioning: a collaborative, supportive approach for women who want to feel listened to, respected and involved in decisions.
  • Online and offline care: hybrid-health programs combine clinic appointments with digital education, movement support and accountability.

Suggestions for improvement

  • Improve price transparency.
  • Build a digital product ladder: low-cost guides, webinars and mini-courses around $19–$49, followed by workshops, consultations and premium multidisciplinary programs.
  • Use Instagram more strategically: recurring video series such as “Ask a Pelvic Physio,” “Menopause Monday,” “Postpartum Recovery Tips,” “Product of the Week” and “What to Expect at Your First Appointment.”
  • Increase social-media reach: Female Physio Co. shows the value of founder-led short-form education, so Winnie Wu and the Papaya team should appear more often in Reels, interviews, Q&A videos and live sessions.
  • Strengthen referral partnerships: build relationships with GPs, obstetricians, gynaecologists, midwives, doulas, fertility clinics, psychologists and local workplaces.
  • Clarify the core brand message: Papaya is not simply another pelvic physiotherapy clinic. Its key promise is coordinated women’s healthcare covering physical, hormonal, nutritional and emotional wellbeing.
